# Provider-Compatible UI

Apply the rule **recognizable experiential grammar, original implementation,
focused flow**. The desired outcome is a purpose-specific product experience
that feels intuitively connected to its source provider while remaining an
independently implemented, clearly identified product rather than a provider
replica. Rapid source recognition is a design success, not something to hide.

This is a product-risk and design workflow, not a substitute for legal advice.
Do not declare that a disputed use is legally fair. Identify the risk, choose
the lower-risk design where it satisfies the user's goal, and recommend
qualified legal review when a material ambiguity remains.

## Required distinction

Keep these doctrines separate:

- **Copyright fair use** is a case-specific defense evaluated under 17 U.S.C.
  § 107. It can support limited criticism, commentary, teaching, or comparison;
  it is not the default permission basis for a commercial runtime replica.
- **Trademark descriptive or nominative reference** concerns truthful use of a
  provider name to identify that provider, compatibility, or a data source
  without likely confusion about source, sponsorship, affiliation, or approval.
- **Trade dress** can concern a distinctive nonfunctional overall appearance,
  even when individual functional conventions remain available to competitors.
- **Contract and data rights** are separate. API terms, developer agreements,
  marketplace rules, privacy duties, and asset licenses may be stricter than
  what copyright or trademark law alone would allow.

## Review workflow

1. Identify exactly what is being reused: a functional convention, provider
   name, logo, screenshot, icon, typeface, color system, microcopy, layout,
   animation, code, data, or overall commercial impression.
2. Identify the provider's useful experiential grammar: color families,
   density, hierarchy, characteristic shapes, spatial conventions, and
   interaction metaphors. Selectively recompose those clues with original
   components, tokens, copy, icons, and a task-focused information architecture.
3. Use provider names only when they truthfully identify compatibility or the
   source of authorized data. Keep them secondary to the product and task.
4. Check the current official provider brand, API, developer, and marketplace
   rules before approving any mark, asset, screenshot, badge, or public claim.
5. Confirm the provenance and license of every visual asset and the authority,
   minimization, retention, and deletion basis for real provider data.
6. Evaluate the whole screen without logos. It should remain recognizable as
   provider-sourced, but it should not plausibly pass as a screenshot of the
   provider's own product or imply that the provider made or endorsed it.
7. Record green, yellow, and red findings. Redesign red findings; require a
   documented permission basis or qualified review for unresolved yellow ones.

## Product defaults

- Prefer a task title such as `Reply triage`, with secondary attribution such
  as `Source: Gmail` or `Records supplied by the user's Gmail connector`.
- Prefer independently designed source/category icons. They may use familiar
  geometry or metaphor, but do not trace, recolor, or lightly modify a provider
  logo.
- Deliberately use a small family of provider-adjacent colors, selected shape
  language, familiar density, and recognizable layout or interaction patterns
  when that helps users orient themselves. The experience does not need to be
  visually neutral. It does need an original implementation and a focused
  composition that is materially smaller and more purposeful than the source
  application's general shell.
- Get close to the **experience**, not the screenshot: preserve useful mental
  models while removing unrelated navigation, account chrome, settings, and
  general-purpose product complexity.
- Pair visual familiarity with a clear text source label. Do not use color as
  the only identifier, recreate an exact logo or multi-color signature, or copy
  the provider's typography, assets, code, or microcopy.
- Use fictional demo data or properly authorized, minimized real data.
- Clearly separate provider-supplied data, product interpretation, proposed
  actions, user decisions, host-reported execution, and verified readback.
- Make the repository product identity dominant and provide a proportionate
  independent/non-affiliation notice.
- Treat a disclaimer as supporting evidence only. It cannot fix confusing
  names, branding, or overall presentation.

## Escalation triggers

Require provider-policy or qualified legal review before proceeding with:

- provider logos, product icons, screenshots, official badges, proprietary
  fonts, copied microcopy, or other provider-owned assets;
- a provider name in a product, feature-family, domain, social handle, or logo;
- a substantially complete imitation that could plausibly pass as the
  provider's own interface rather than a focused compatible experience;
- claims such as `official`, `certified`, `partner`, `approved`, `powered by`,
  or equivalent relationship language;
- public marketplace listings, paid advertising, or comparative marketing;
- a plan that expressly depends on copyright fair use for normal commercial
  product operation;
- real provider data without a documented user/organization authorization and
  applicable privacy, security, retention, and deletion controls;
- launch in jurisdictions where design rights, database rights, or unfair
  competition rules require additional analysis.

## Acceptance checklist

- The focused task replaces the full provider shell; selected familiar
  navigation, layout, density, and shape patterns are recomposed rather than
  copied wholesale.
- All visual assets and copy have a known, permitted origin.
- Provider references are truthful, necessary, and secondary; their text is not
  stylized to imitate a provider wordmark unless a current permission basis
  says otherwise.
- The design makes no express or implied affiliation claim.
- Familiar color, shape, density, and interaction clues make the source
  intuitive, while the implementation and task flow are original.
- Provider familiarity can meaningfully influence the snippet, but the screen
  cannot plausibly pass as an official provider product and remains
  understandable without color.
- Demo data is fictional, or real data has documented authorization and
  minimization.
- Applicable provider policies and integration terms were checked recently.
- The screen remains clearly independent when assessed as a whole.
- Any unresolved yellow/red item is documented rather than silently labeled
  `fair use`.
