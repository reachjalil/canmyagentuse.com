# Product-first research and commercialization

Updated: 2026-09-04. This is the current product direction, authorized by the
site owner. It supersedes the harness-only prioritization in the historical
research mission while retaining every existing collection and assertion.

## Positioning

Can My Agent Use helps a person choose a service their agent can actually use.
The entry point is a job or product category, not a protocol glossary. The
answer follows the whole journey: establish an account, authorize access,
provision what is needed, understand paid requirements, execute a bounded
useful action, and verify its result.

The durable advantage is a dated, inspectable action record. A directory of
MCP endpoints alone cannot answer whether an agent can establish an account,
use the right scopes, complete payment, or deliver a real result. The existing
harness corpus is the second axis: which agent surface can use the route?

## First release

13 guides: Cloudflare, Gmail, Salesforce, Notion, Obsidian, OneNote, GitHub,
Slack, Linear, Atom Tickets, AMC Theatres, Best Buy, and Amazon. Every entry
records four account/task questions and five interface checks. The product
finder filters by category and all required interfaces, including an
official-only option. MCP tools and MCP Apps are separate fields.

A documented route is not a live test. The initial release contains no new
live account creation or financial transaction tests. Unknown means evidence
is absent or insufficient, not that the product is incapable. The Amazon
entry concerns Amazon's own shopping assistant and explicitly does not claim
that a generic outside agent can check out.

## Product presentation and submissions

The owner requested a commercially attractive, minimal directory on 2026-09-04.
Keep the first view focused on product identity, one short use case, one primary
action status, and compact connection badges. For shopping and movie tickets,
the primary action is purchasing; a discovery API does not earn a ready
checkout status. Preserve full evidence, account setup, and research in guide
pages and expandable sections rather than adding those details to every card.

Keep a prominent `/submit-app` entry point. Submissions enter the existing
editorial review queue with a trackable receipt, never a published listing or
support claim. Require app identity and evidence; keep optional contact private.
Submission is free and separate from sponsorship. Real product marks remain
secondary, locally served references governed by the provider manifest.

## Research order

1. Deepen the existing SaaS records: precise permission scopes, entry-level
   plan requirements, regional constraints, revocation, rate limits, and a
   source-backed first-task walkthrough. Add exact-action rows when four
   journey questions are too coarse.
2. Expand tech SaaS by category: Vercel, Netlify, Supabase, Neon, Stripe,
   Sentry, Figma, Airtable, Asana, Trello, HubSpot, Google Drive, Calendar,
   Microsoft Teams, Dropbox, Box, and Zendesk. These names are research leads,
   not claims of support.
3. Deepen notes and knowledge: compare Notion, Obsidian, OneNote, Joplin,
   Evernote, and other relevant services using the same criteria. Verify
   official vs community provenance and separate local from remote access.
4. Add consumer research with a concrete finish line: movie tickets first,
   then retail shopping, travel, reservations, and delivery. Record whether
   the agent merely links out, prepares checkout, submits an order, or verifies
   fulfillment. Read relevant access restrictions before making claims.
5. Improve discoverability: stable category landing pages and their Markdown
   twins, use-case guides, exact task comparisons, source freshness views,
   portable filters, and product-aware canonical agent prompts.

Do not flood the publication with thin records to hit a count. Prefer a
bounded batch of 2–4 well-sourced additions or substantial improvements per
research cycle. Publish editorial articles when a real finding explains an
interesting task. The movie-ticket article is the initial example.

## Evidence contract

- Prefer official provider documentation. For community implementations,
  use the maintainer's source and identify that maintainer explicitly.
- Record review dates, exact route scope, prerequisites, and source links.
- Review signup, connection, purchase, and work separately. A scratch org is
  not a new paid Salesforce org; a CRM Account record is not a login.
- Separate an MCP server, an MCP host, and MCP Apps. Host-side rendering
  support does not prove the service's server exposes an interactive app.
- API discovery, affiliate links, public documentation, and provider-native
  assistants do not establish general third-party purchasing access.
- Do not execute third-party signup, reserved seating, payments, subscription
  purchases, or messages as part of an unattended documentation cycle. A live
  test needs its own task-specific authorization, environment, and receipt.
- Source disagreement and access restrictions belong in the published answer.

## Commercial model

The owner confirmed OpenWork as the first founding sponsor on 2026-09-04 and
explicitly authorized a labeled public placement. The first placement uses
plain text, a sponsored outbound link, and no third-party ad network script.
The relationship is disclosed in `/sponsors`, `/about`, and the movie article.
OpenWork is also covered by the harness catalog; disclose that overlap.

Offer four products: founding sponsorship, category advertising, disclosed
research underwriting, and labeled demonstrations. Sell defined placements
and deliverables with a time period. Never sell support labels, organic
ranking, favorable outcomes, removal of competitors, or editorial veto.

Before quoting traffic-based rates, establish real first-party audience
measurement. Keep a media kit with dated, sourced product/category counts,
measured reach when available, inventory, sample placement, deliverables,
term, reporting, and editorial rules. No invented audience or conversion
claims. Use public inquiries to begin contact, then agree confidential terms
privately; no unsolicited outbound messages are authorized by this mission.

Future recurring revenue can come from freshness alerts, team shortlists,
change feeds, and contracted research. Review data-license obligations before
selling redistribution or API access. Keep basic evidence and corrections
public so commercial offerings add convenience rather than distort findings.

## Shipping loop

Use the existing `continue-catalog-coverage` automation updated to this product
mission, not a duplicate harness-only publisher. Before each cycle, fetch
origin/main and inspect the tree. Do not overwrite another task's changes or
deploy an older checkout over a newer release. If needed, use an isolated
worktree and integrate only cycle-owned changes.

Run the narrowest checks, Biome on changed supported files, then `pnpm check`,
`pnpm test`, and `pnpm build`. Verify the rendered changed pages, filters,
Markdown/JSON parity, sources, navigation, and social assets. Push only a
validated fast-forward release to main; deploy through the existing Workers
configuration. Read back production routes with a cache-busting query and
verify the Worker version. Record the release and next research items in the
ops ledger. Never mark a deployment successful from a local build alone.

Stay quiet if a scheduled cycle has no meaningful change. Notify for a
published batch, material failure, or input required to proceed.
